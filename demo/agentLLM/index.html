Agent Evaluation.

I’m a fourth-year PhD candidate in trustworthy LLMs at the University of Bristol, and previously completed a research placement at the Alan Turing Institute.

My research focuses on how LLMs are trained and evaluated. On the training side, I study how multiple objectives interfere with or transfer to one another during post-training. On the evaluation side, I study how to assess a model's reasoning process, from individual steps to multi-agent interactions.

MONICA: Real-Time Monitoring and Calibration of Chain-of-Thought Sycophancy

Reasoning models give in to a user’s wrong hint partway through their chain of thought. MONICA puts linear probes on hidden states, scores sycophantic drift every three sentences, and steers the activations back when the score passes 0.5.

Preprint 2026 · First author

LogicTrack: Auditing Reasoning Trajectories with Formal Logic Solvers

Models often reach the right answer through invalid steps. LogicTrack formalises each step into SMT-LIB, checks it with Z3 during generation, and backtracks when the solver-based reward falls below 0.8. A LoRA-tuned variant learns to emit <backtrack> itself.

Algorithms 2025 · First author

						
SHARP: Mitigating Degree Bias with Hard-to-Learn Nodes

						
Graph models do worst on nodes with few neighbours. SHARP’s contrastive loss takes positives from labels and adaptively up-weights hard negatives, so poorly connected nodes get a cleaner training signal.

					

					

						

							
							

						

						

							
F1 by node degree · Cora, GAT vs SHARP (Table 9) · axis from 0.6

							

							
GATSHARP

							
The largest gain is on degree-1 nodes (0.708 → 0.827). With half the labels, SHARP-GCN still beats GCN trained on all of them.

							
Adaptive weighting directs more training signal to hard, underserved examples, which can strengthen a multi-path scheme.

ACL 2026 Findings · Second author

						
AutoMonitor-Bench: How Reliable Are LLM Misbehaviour Monitors?

						
3,010 paired samples across three categories (safety, sycophancy and specification gaming), used to test 22 LLM monitors by miss rate and false-alarm rate. Monitors are also fine-tuned with LoRA on a 153K-sample training set.

								→
								
MonitorQwen3 + LoRA
r = 16 · 1 × A100

							

							

							
Miss rate on specification gaming (Tables 9–10, Fig. 8)

							

						

						

							
On its own category the tuned monitor becomes near-perfect (Qwen3-4B: miss rate 0.59 → 0.00). On a different category it gets much worse. Across monitors, miss rate and false-alarm rate trade off (r = −0.54).

							
This is a direct measurement of negative transfer: one adapter improves its target category while weakening performance elsewhere. Multi-policy training is designed to contain that spillover.

NeurIPS 2026 · Second author

						
StakeBench: Language Understanding Grounded in Market Commitment

						
560,876 prediction-market comments, labelled not by annotators but by what the speaker actually bet. Four tasks of increasing difficulty test 15 LLMs, run on GH200 nodes through Slurm.

					

					

						

							

							
G3 · predicted action within 48 h (Table 4)

							

							

							

								
G1Who holds a position?

								
G2Which side is revealed?

								
G3What action follows?

								
G4Where do odds move?

							

						

						

							
10 of 15 models recall only one or two of the four action classes, and no model beats the stake-weighted baseline on G4 overall. The best model (GPT-5.5) reaches only 0.599 directed accuracy on G2.

							
Per-class and per-split evaluation exposes collapse hidden by aggregate scores and shows whether a new training path delivers a genuine gain.
