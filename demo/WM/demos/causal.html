Demo 2 · Causal Consistency

Can a Model Keep the Rules as the World Grows?

A generated world may seem to run fine and still let a player skip a required step. We test whether the causal rules in a world's description are followed when it is built, and maintained when it is extended.

Motivation

One Rule, One New Request.

A wet road. Round 1 builds the world with a truck; round 2 adds a motorcycle. The motorcycle can cross in both round-2 versions, but only one still makes you dry the road first.

Near side
Far side

Rule from Round 1

Any vehicle crossing the wet road skids, so no vehicle can reach the far side until the road has been dried.

Illustrative mini-world written by hand for this page. It is a simplified version of the wet-floor task, not a model output.

Research Questions

RQ1

Build

How often can a model generate a causally consistent world? The goal must be reachable, the required states present, and every stated relation must really constrain play.

RQ2

Extend

Can a model add new requests and keep the old rules? Requests are added in parallel, each to the same initial world, or in series, each on top of the last. A rule stays in force unless a request explicitly revises it.

RQ3

Self-Improve

Can self-evolution close the gap? For example, with recursive self-improvement (RSI), the model reads the verifier's counterexamples and revises its own worlds, prompts or tools. We measure whether rule-keeping improves from one iteration to the next.

Views

Four Models, One Broken Rule.

One request from the serial run that every model got wrong. Each model first built the body shop and passed. Then it was asked to add a robot paint cell. All four cells paint a bare panel in one step, so the panel is never primed. Pick a model to see its code, which rules it broke, and the steps that break them.

Round 1 · The World

A body shop. One bare car panel stands on a trestle, and an extractor fan runs in the roof when it is switched on. Goal: the panel is painted.

Round 1 · How It Is Solved

panel bare · extractor off → switch extractor on → bare · on → spray (primer) → primed · on → spray (paint) → painted · on

    Results
    
		
    
			
    RQ2 · Extend
    
			
    We test two settings.
    
			
    Parallel. Different requests are each added once, independently, to the same initial world.
    
			
    Serial. Requests pile up over many rounds, each built on the previous world. We track the first broken rule, new breaks per round, and whether earlier features survive.
    
			
    

			
    Parallel vs Serial
    
			
    The same ten requests per scene, asked one at a time on the round-1 world (parallel) or stacked one on another (serial). Every model is less often correct and breaks more rules once the requests stack: gpt-6-luna goes from 93% to 61% correct, and from 4% to 23% of requests breaking a rule.

    Serial, Round by Round
    
			
    The two stronger models start near 80% correct and fall to under half by round 10. They keep earlier features: what they lose is the old rules. The two weaker models rarely get a usable world past round 1.
    
			
    
			
    
				
    Correct Rate at Round k
    Scenes where, after round k, every feature added so far works and every rule holds, over all 30 scenes.
    
				
    Violation Rate up to Round k
    Requests 1 to k that left a rule broken, over all requests 1 to k. The value at round 10 is the run's overall rate.
    
				
    Retention Rate at Round k
    Scenes where every feature from rounds 1 to k−1 still works, over the scenes that can be judged at round k.
    
				
    Scenes Correct in Every Round So Far
    Scenes whose rounds 1 to k are all correct, over all 30 scenes. Once a scene slips, it stays out.
